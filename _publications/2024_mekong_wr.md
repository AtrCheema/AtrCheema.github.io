Salt is spreading through the groundwater of the Mekong Delta, where millions of people depend on wells
for drinking water and irrigation. Rather than reduce the problem to a single number, this work predicted
three separate chemical signatures of salt across the delta's stacked layers of groundwater, comparing
nine models built on field measurements. Chloride, the clearest signature, was predicted well enough to
explain 94% of the variation, while the two subtler indicators proved harder, at 78% and 67%. Opening the
models up to see what drove each prediction revealed that the dominant cause of the salt changes with both
the indicator and the depth, and that the volume of water people pump out came through strongly for
several of them. That is what makes the results usable for deciding where to intervene, rather than only
for forecasting where salt will show up next.
