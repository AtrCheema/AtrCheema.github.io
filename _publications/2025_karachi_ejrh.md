Groundwater beneath Karachi is falling by half a metre to a metre every year, and the sea has already
pushed 6.41 kilometres inland to fill the space it leaves. Pakistan's largest city draws heavily on water
stored underground right against the Arabian Sea, and once saltwater reaches a well, that well is
finished. A computer model of the city's groundwater, calibrated against years of well readings, rainfall
records and land use change, was used here to test four plausible futures. Under the mildest, the salt
front reaches 8.1 kilometres inland by 2043; under the harshest, combining reduced rainfall with doubled
pumping, it reaches 17.78 kilometres and overtakes major urban and industrial districts. Those distances
and dates turn a familiar warning into a schedule, and make the case for capping extraction and
deliberately replenishing the water underground while that is still worth doing.
