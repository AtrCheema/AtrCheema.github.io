2,4-D is among the world's most heavily used herbicides and it persists in wastewater, where one route to
destroying it is to shine light on it in the presence of a catalyst. Testing every combination of catalyst
recipe and operating condition at the bench is impossibly slow, so 1,050 measurements on bismuth ferrite
catalysts — including versions seeded with gold — were pooled and used to train models predicting how much
herbicide each combination destroys. Weighing fourteen variables against one another, the models found
that how the process is run mattered more than the fine chemical make-up of the catalyst itself. The study
also reports the settings that gave the highest breakdown: a catalyst dose of 1.5 g/L, a light intensity
of 105 W, neutral acidity, and a starting herbicide concentration of 5 mg/L.
